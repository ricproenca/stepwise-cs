## 7.1 Ethics as a Computing Professional

Ethics are principles that help determine right from wrong in professional situations. Computing ethics guide professionals to create fair, honest, and non-harmful technology.

**Why ethics matter:** ensures software safety and reliability, protects user data and privacy, maintains professional credibility, prevents legal violations, supports fair treatment of all users.

### Professional Ethical Bodies

**BCS (British Computer Society)** and **IEEE** establish standards for computing professionals. Membership provides: ethical guidelines, professional credibility, support during dilemmas, continuing education.

## IEEE-ACM Software Engineering Code of Ethics: Eight Principles

| # | Principle | Key Obligation | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Public** | Act in society's best interest, not just profit | Accurate healthcare apps; protect social media data; avoid AI bias |
| 2 | **Client & Employer** | Respect interests while maintaining integrity | Report security flaws; refuse to exaggerate product capabilities |
| 3 | **Product** | Ensure high-quality, safe, tested software | Extensive self-driving car testing; secure e-commerce platforms |
| 4 | **Judgment** | Make ethical decisions based on what's right | Fix AI bias; refuse unethical requests (spyware, unfair pricing) |
| 5 | **Management** | Promote ethical work environments | No software piracy; fair compensation; strong data protection |
| 6 | **Profession** | Advance the profession's integrity | Teach ethical hacking; transparent AI research; report misconduct |
| 7 | **Colleagues** | Be fair and supportive to team members | Mentor junior developers; credit contributions; report harassment |
| 8 | **Self** | Continue learning throughout career | Attend cybersecurity workshops; study AI ethics; learn inclusive design |

## 7.2 Acting Ethically and Consequences

### Benefits of Ethical Behaviour

- **Trust** — builds credibility with employers, clients, and users
- **Legal compliance** — avoids fines, lawsuits, and imprisonment
- **Reputation** — demonstrates professionalism and responsibility
- **Fairness** — prevents discrimination in systems and software
- **Social responsibility** — ensures work benefits society without causing harm

### Consequences of Unethical Behaviour

| Consequence | Example |
|---|---|
| Loss of trust | Clients abandon companies that act dishonestly |
| Legal penalties | Fines, lawsuits, imprisonment for data/copyright violations |
| Reputation damage | Difficult to rebuild after public scandals |
| Financial loss | Legal fees, compensation, expensive system repairs |
| Harm to individuals/society | Biased AI discriminates; faulty medical software endangers patients |

## 7.3 Copyright Legislation

Copyright gives creators exclusive legal rights to control how their work is used, shared, or distributed.

**Why copyright laws are needed:**

1. **Protecting creator's work** — prevents unauthorised copying or modification
2. **Encouraging innovation** — provides financial incentives for development
3. **Preventing software piracy** — makes illegal copying a criminal offence
4. **Ensuring fair use** — allows limited educational and research use while protecting creators
5. **Legal accountability** — enables creators to pursue violators

## 7.4 Software Licensing

| Licence | Description | Source Code | Cost | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FSF (Free Software Foundation)** | Four freedoms: use, study/modify, distribute, share modifications | Freely available | Free | Linux |
| **OSI (Open Source Initiative)** | Source accessible; community contributions welcome; more flexible than FSF | Accessible | Free or paid | Firefox |
| **Shareware** | Trial basis with time/feature limits; pay for full access | Closed | Trial free; full paid | WinRAR |
| **Commercial (Proprietary)** | Sold under paid licence; source inaccessible; no modification/redistribution | Closed | Paid | Microsoft Office |

**Choosing a licence based on:**
- Software purpose (profit vs. community benefit)
- Level of control needed (open vs. protected source)
- Target audience (developers, businesses, general public)
- Financial goals

## 7.5 Artificial Intelligence (AI)

### Social Impact

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| Improved accessibility (speech recognition) | Job displacement in automation-heavy industries |
| Faster disease diagnosis | Bias and inequality from biased training data |
| Personalised learning in education | Privacy violations through facial recognition |

### Economic Impact

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| Increased productivity through automation | Job losses especially in manufacturing |
| Cost savings via efficiency improvements | High implementation costs for small businesses |
| New jobs in AI development and data science | Wider economic gap between rich and poor |

### Environmental Impact

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| Smart grids optimise energy use | Data centres consume enormous energy |
| Climate prediction models inform conservation | E-waste from quickly obsolete AI devices |
| Optimised processes reduce emissions | Environmental damage from hardware material mining |

### Major Applications

| Sector | Application |
|---|---|
| Healthcare | Disease diagnosis; personalised treatment planning |
| Finance | Fraud detection; market prediction |
| Education | Adaptive learning systems; performance-based customisation |
| Transportation | Autonomous vehicles; traffic management |
| Entertainment | Content recommendations; realistic graphics generation |
